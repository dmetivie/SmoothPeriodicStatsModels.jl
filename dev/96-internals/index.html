An AbstractHMM that reads its emission loglikelihoods from a precomputed matrix. It takes initialization, transition_matrix and obs_distributions from model, and the emission loglikelihoods from LL, an N × K matrix computed beforehand (for example with loglikelihoods!). SmoothPeriodicStatsModels.OptimMLE — Type
OptimMLE(ℓ, solver, θ₀)

A maximum likelihood problem stated directly as a loss to minimize, for models the package has no dedicated fit_mle for.

  • ℓ(θ, y) is the negative loglikelihood, taking the parameters and the data
  • solver is the algorithm handed to solve, which can still be overridden per call
  • θ₀ is the starting point

The solver stored here is a default, not a constraint: fit_mle(opt, y; solver = …) wins.

SmoothPeriodicStatsModels.RobustEmissions — Type
RobustEmissions(model)

An AbstractHMM that keeps the forward pass finite when an observation is impossible. It takes everything from model, but gives an observation that is impossible in every state the same small loglikelihood, log(floatmin()) ≈ -708, in all of them, and caps an infinite loglikelihood at log(floatmax()). The fits run their E-step on it with robust = true.

SeasonalFamily(dists, θ, fam, design)

One component of a seasonal mixture during an EM fit. It holds the T distributions it takes over a period, the coefficients θ that generate them, its seasonal_family fam, and design, that family's bases evaluated over the period.

SmoothPeriodicStatsModels.SeasonalWeights — Type
SeasonalWeights(α, θ, basis, cfg)

The seasonal mixing weights of a mixture, as the weight vector the EM algorithm of ExpectationMaximization works on.

α is K × T: α[k, t] is the weight of component k at period index t. θ holds the (K-1) × ncoef multinomial-logit coefficients that generate it, with component K as the reference, and basis is the T × ncoef design matrix of the weights' basis. cfg holds the M-step settings.

Indexing it returns the time-averaged weight of a component.

SmoothPeriodicStatsModels.bernoulli_logit_nll — Method
bernoulli_logit_nll(θ, (γ, basis)) -> Number

Negative of the seasonal Bernoulli loglikelihood maximized by the emission M-step.

γ[t, 1] and γ[t, 2] are the posterior weights of a failure and of a success at period index t, and the success probability is p(t) = logistic(P(t)) with P = basis * θ — the convention of αₜ and Trig2ARPeriodicHMM. The objective is then -Σₜ γ[t,1] log(1 - p(t)) + γ[t,2] log p(t).

SmoothPeriodicStatsModels.exponential_nll — Method
exponential_nll(θ, (s_π, s_πy, basis)) -> Number

Negative of the seasonal Exponential loglikelihood maximized by the mixture component M-step.

With scale λ(t) = exp(P(t)), P = basis * θ, the loglikelihood of the observations attached to period index t is -s_π[t] P(t) - s_πy[t] exp(-P(t)), where s_π[t] is the summed posterior weight and s_πy[t] the posterior-weighted sum of the observations.

Fit in place a mixture of product-Bernoulli laws to the observations of one slice. The weights α and the Bernoulli emissions B (K × D × 2^order, or K × D vectors for site-dependent memory) are fitted to Y, site j of observation n using memory category lag_cat[j, n]. SmoothPeriodicStatsModels.norm_cdf_2d_vfast — Method
norm_cdf_2d_vfast(x₁, x₂, ρ)

Compute an approximation of the bivariate normal CDF with zero means, unit variances and correlation ρ at the point (x₁, x₂):

∫{-∞}^{x₁} ∫{-∞}^{x₂} φ(u, v; ρ) du dv

where φ = (1/(2π√(1-ρ²))) * exp(-1/(2(1-ρ²))(u² - 2ρuv + v²)) is the bivariate normal density function with correlation ρ.

Tsay, Wen-Jen, and Peng-Hsuan Ke. "A simple approximation for the bivariate normal integral." Communications in Statistics-Simulation and Computation (2021): 1-14.

Converted from https://github.com/david-cortes/approxcdf

SmoothPeriodicStatsModels.pairwise_loglikelihood — Method
pairwise_loglikelihood(R, B, h, Y, wp, n_pair; n2t, eps = 1e-10, pairwise_indices2) -> Float64

The weighted pairwise (composite) loglikelihood of the spatial Bernoulli emission of one hidden state, without memory. R (length T) is the range and B (T × D) the marginal success probabilities at each period index, h the D × D distances. For each pair (i, j) with weight wp[i, j] > 0, n_pair[s, i, j, t] counts the observations at period index t in each of the four joint outcomes s (11, 10, 01, 00); the bivariate probabilities come from norm_cdf_2d_vfast and are floored at eps.

SmoothPeriodicStatsModels.polynomial_trigo — Method
polynomial_trigo(t, β) -> Number

Trigonometric polynomial of degree d = (length(β) - 1) ÷ 2 evaluated at t, where t is expressed in fractions of a period: t and t + 1 give the same value.

\[β₁ + \sum_{l=1}^{d} β_{2l} \cos(2π l t) + β_{2l+1} \sin(2π l t)\]

A degree-0 β gives the constant β[1].

SmoothPeriodicStatsModels.update_B! — Method

SmoothPeriodicStatsModels.update_R! — Method
update_R!(hmm::ARPeriodicHMMSpatial, θᴿ, γ, wp, Y, SituationIdx; n2t, basis, solver, adtype, maxiters = 10, solve_kwargs)

M-step for the spatial ranges of the hidden states. For each state k, it weights the joint outcomes of each pair of sites by the posteriors γ[:, k], refits θᴿ[k, :] with fit_mle_one_R!, and writes the resulting ranges into hmm.R. SituationIdx gives the joint-outcome category of every pair and observation; basis is the design matrix of the range, trigonometric by default; maxiters caps the solver iterations. The states are refitted in parallel threads.

SmoothPeriodicStatsModels.update_dist — Method
update_dist(fam, design, θ, γ, observations, n_in_t; solver, adtype, solve_kwargs)

M-step for one seasonal family: a weighted maximum likelihood fit. It refits the coefficients θ of the family fam to the observations weighted by their posteriors γ, and returns (distributions, θ), the family's nT distributions and its coefficients. A mixture component and an emission block of a SmoothPeriodicHMM are fitted this way. design is the family's bases over the period and n_in_t[t] lists the observations at period index t.

The generic method maximizes the weighted loglikelihood, so any family with a seasonal_family declaration works. A method for a specific family can replace it, for example to use sufficient statistics, as the Exponential method does:

SmoothPeriodicStatsModelsOptimizationExt.fit_mle_one_R! — Method
fit_mle_one_R!(θᴿ, B, h, Y, wp, n_pair; n2t, basis, solver, adtype, solve_kwargs) -> θᴿ

Refit in place the spatial range coefficients θᴿ of one hidden state. The range at period index t is exp(basis[t, :] ⋅ θᴿ), and the fit maximizes the weighted pairwise loglikelihood (pairwise_loglikelihood, or pairwise_loglikelihood_memory when B, the T × D × 2^order marginal probabilities, has memory). n_pair holds the posterior-weighted counts of joint outcomes and wp the pair weights. basis, the T × length(θᴿ) design matrix, defaults to the trigonometric one.
