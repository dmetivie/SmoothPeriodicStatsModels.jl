HiddenMarkovModels.jl

The hidden Markov models of this package are all subtype HiddenMarkovModels.AbstractHMM from HiddenMarkovModels.jl: SmoothARPeriodicHMM, SmoothsARPeriodicHMM, SmoothARPeriodicHMMSpatial, SmoothPeriodicHMM and their evaluated twins . The forward and backward passes of every fit and likelihood here are that package's.

A periodic HMM is a controlled HMM

HiddenMarkovModels.jl lets the transitions and the emissions depend on a control, a known input at each step; its documentation calls this time dependency. A periodic autoregressive HMM is the case where the control at step n is

(t, h, n, s) = (n2t[n], lag_cat[:, n], n, n2t[n-1])
  • t, the period index, chooses the emission laws;
  • h[j], the memory category of series j, encodes its order previous values, which the emission of series j depends on;
  • n is the index of the step;
  • s is the period index of the previous step. HiddenMarkovModels.jl asks for the transition matrix of the step that arrives at a control, while here A[:, :, t] is the step that leaves period index t, so the control carries s.

SeasonalControls builds that control sequence from the data, without storing the tuples:

using SmoothPeriodicStatsModels, Randomimport HiddenMarkovModels as HMMsRandom.seed!(2)K, D, order, T = 2, 3, 1, 20m = SmoothARPeriodicHMM([0.5, 0.5], randn(K, K - 1, 3), randn(K, D, 2^order, 3), T)n2t = n_to_t(200, T)Y_past = zeros(Bool, D, order)Y = rand(m, n2t; Y_past)controls = SeasonalControls(Y, Y_past; n2t)controls[2]
(2, [1, 1, 2], 2, 1)

What applies

The inference functions of HiddenMarkovModels.jl take the observations as a vector, one entry per step, and the controls:

obs = [Y[:, n] for n in axes(Y, 2)]HMMs.logdensityof(m, obs, controls) ≈ loglikelihood(m, Y, Y_past; n2t)
true

forward_backward gives the posterior probabilities of the states, viterbi the most likely path, forward the filtered probabilities. For several sequences, lay them end to end and pass seq_ends, the index of the last step of each:

Ys = [rand(m, 1:T; Y_past) for _ in 1:5]Y_pasts = fill(Y_past, 5)n2ts = fill(1:T, 5)obs = [Y[:, n] for Y in Ys for n in axes(Y, 2)]controls = SeasonalControls(Ys, Y_pasts; n2t=n2ts)seq_ends = cumsum(size.(Ys, 2))γ, logL = HMMs.forward_backward(m, obs, controls; seq_ends)logL   # one loglikelihood per sequence

Two functions do not apply:

  • baum_welch. Fit with fit_mle, which runs Baum-Welch with the forward-backward of HiddenMarkovModels.jl and the M-step of this package, and takes the same data layout.
  • rand. Its controls are fixed in advance, so the memory categories would come from given data rather than from the values just drawn. rand(m, n2t; Y_past) simulates the model.

Models without memory

SmoothPeriodicHMM has no memory, so its control holds the period index alone, with empty memory categories: SeasonalControls(n2t) builds it. Nothing in the controls then depends on the observations, and rand from HiddenMarkovModels.jl applies too:

using Distributionsm = SmoothPeriodicHMM(Normal, 2, 1, T; init=[(μ=0.0, σ=1.0), (μ=3.0, σ=1.0)])controls = SeasonalControls(n2t)states, obs = HMMs.rand(m, controls)HMMs.logdensityof(m, obs, controls) ≈ loglikelihood(m, reduce(hcat, obs); n2t)
true

For the spatial model, every emission loglikelihood is a quasi-Monte-Carlo estimate. The functions of HiddenMarkovModels.jl work, but they evaluate one step at a time, while loglikelihood and the fits evaluate all steps at once, in parallel.

PeriodicHiddenMarkovModels.jl

PeriodicHiddenMarkovModels.jl is the non-smooth relative: one transition matrix and one set of emission laws per period index, any emission distribution, no autoregressive memory. It suits data with many observations at every period index. Here the parameters are smooth functions of the period index, which needs far fewer observations and keeps the meaning of the states over the cycle. SmoothPeriodicHMM is its smooth counterpart. One difference: there, the transition matrix of a period index is that of the step arriving at it; here, that of the step leaving it, which differs when n2t skips an index.
