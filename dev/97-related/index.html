Related approaches

This page compares the package with the regression models it builds on, GLMs, GAMs and GAMLSS, and lists packages that fit hidden Markov models and mixtures with covariates.

GLMs, GAMs and GAMLSS

For one parameter of independent data, a model of this package is a generalized linear model (GLM) whose covariates are the basis functions $b_i(t_n)$. The probability of rain on the home page, a trigonometric polynomial of degree 2 through the logistic function, is the logistic regression

glm(rain ~ cos(2*pi*t/T) + sin(2*pi*t/T) + cos(4*pi*t/T) + sin(4*pi*t/T), family = binomial)

in R, or with GLM.jl on the columns of design_matrix(TrigBasis(T, 2), n2t), and has the same maximum-likelihood coefficients. This package finds them with an Optimization.jl solver instead of iteratively reweighted least squares. In the same way:

  • IndicatorBasis(month_of, 1:12) is a month factor;
  • CyclicBSplineBasis(T, q) is a cyclic regression spline, as bs = "cc" in mgcv, without its penalty;
  • a model whose parameters are all smooth, such as the mean and the standard deviation of a Normal or both parameters of a Gamma, is a distributional regression, as in GAMLSS;
  • each parameter takes its own basis, and bases add up, as the terms of a formula do.

What this package adds

  • Hidden states. The weights of a mixture, the transitions of a hidden Markov model and the emission of each state are smooth, and fitted together by EM. Each M-step is a weighted fit of the regressions above, weighted by the posterior probabilities of the states.
  • Parameters that are not the mean of a response: the autoregressive coefficient $\rho(t)$ of SmoothAR1, through an interval link; each row of a transition matrix, through a softmax; the ranges of the spatial Gaussian copula of SmoothARPeriodicHMMSpatial.
  • Any law of Distributions.jl. A mixture component or an emission block can follow any law declared as a seasonal family, one method of seasonal_family, including multivariate laws with a smooth correlation and copulas. GLM software is limited to exponential families, and GAMLSS mostly to univariate laws.
  • Any optimizer. Every fit takes any Optimization.jl solver and automatic differentiation backend (see the fitting guide), where GLM and GAM software fix their algorithm.
  • Simulation. rand draws from the fitted model along the cycle, as a weather generator needs.

What GLMs and GAMs offer that this package does not (yet)

  • Penalized smoothness. A GAM takes many basis functions and penalizes their roughness, with the amount of smoothing estimated by REML or GCV. This package has no penalty: a curve is smooth because its basis is small, and the degree or the number of knots is chosen by comparing fits by BIC (see the fitting guide). The fit stays a plain maximum likelihood, but the choice is discrete and made by hand.
  • Covariates. The parameters depend on the period index only, and on the previous observations in the models with memory. A GLM takes any covariate, such as a temperature or a climate index.
  • Inference on the coefficients. There are no standard errors, confidence intervals or tests; the likelihood, AIC and BIC compare models.

Hidden Markov models and mixtures with covariates

Several R packages let the parameters of a hidden Markov model or of a mixture depend on covariates. With the basis functions of the period index as covariates, they fit periodic models too.

  • depmixS4: hidden Markov models whose transitions are multinomial logistic regressions on covariates, and whose emissions are GLMs.
  • momentuHMM: multivariate hidden Markov models for animal movement, with a cosinor(cov, period) term for cyclical covariates on the transitions.
  • hmmTMB: hidden Markov models with smoothing splines and random effects on the transitions and the emission parameters, fitted with Template Model Builder. It is the closest to this package, with penalized smoothness.
  • flexmix: mixtures of regressions fitted by EM, whose weights can depend on covariates (concomitant variables).

This package is built around the cycle: the period indices n2t, curves over $1, \dots, T$, autoregressive Bernoulli emissions, spatially correlated emissions and simulation, in Julia, on HiddenMarkovModels.jl.
